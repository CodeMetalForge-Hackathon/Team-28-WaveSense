What this does not show

  • A candidate means look here, not a confirmed person. No candidate is not evidence that nobody is there.
  • 24 GHz passes wood, cardboard, plastic and fabric; it is blocked by concrete, brick, stone and metal. This is not a test of rubble.
  • One radar gives range, not direction or count. Anyone moving within ~2 m of the radar, on either side of the door, also triggers it.
  • One room, one person, one evening. No long quiet-room false-alarm rate has been measured yet. Not validated for life-safety use.
  • Rough inference: with the door, the person read at ~1.5 m against ~0.9 m without it, and the signal stayed far above threshold, so the wooden door cost far less signal than the extra distance alone would predict. Not measured directly.

Hardware: Analog Devices EV-TINYRAD24G (24.00–24.25 GHz FMCW, 4 receivers, 0.6 m range resolution) driven by ADI's TinyRadTool code; ESP32-C5 Wi-Fi CSI node with an external antenna. Code: branch wifi-xray/near-zone-presence, folders wifi-sensing/radar and wifi-sensing/xray.
